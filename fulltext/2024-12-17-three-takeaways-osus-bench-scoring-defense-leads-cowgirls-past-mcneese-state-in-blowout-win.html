Three takeaways: OSU's bench scoring, defense leads Cowgirls past McNeese State in blowout win

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2024-12-17
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Oklahoma State closed out its nonconference schedule on a high note, beating McNeese State 92-41 at Gallager-Iba Arena on Monday.

Despite it being a blowout, the Cowgirls had a tougher time than the scoreboard showed. OSU had to find ways to continue playing winning basketball, and it did, winning a third straight game.

Here are three takeaways from the game:

Defensive pressure on both sides

The game started slowly for both sides with a halftime score of 34-16. OSU and McNeese's combined 3-point percentage was 30%, a big part of a slow-scoring back-and-forth caused by both offensive mistakes and sound defense.

McNeese temporarily slowed OSU’s offense by playing in lengthy zones, making shots difficult. On the other side, OSU pressed for a lot of the game, a departure from their usual style of defense.

Associate head coach Jhasmine Player said the Cowgirls executed their different defensive schemes well.

“It's something we've been working on for a while, “Player said. “We've played it in quite a few games. I think today was the day you saw it generate the most stops”

The Cowgirl’s defense was able to hold MSU to 23% field goal percentage, 18 lower than their season average.

With the absence of Anna Gret Asi and some early shooting struggles from the starting lineup, the Cowgirl’s bench was able to step up for the team, contributing 44 points to the blowout.

OSU freshman guard Jadyn Wooten was a major contributor, racking up 16 points and a team-high 6 assists. Wooten’s ability to distribute the ball greatly bolstered the Cowgirl’s offense.

5th-year player Stacie Jones also stepped up, leading OSU with 18 points from the bench. Jones went 7 for 8 from the field to go along with a perfect 4 for 4 on free throws.

“I just felt like I was doing my job,” Jones said. “Each and every one of us have a role, and that is to contribute when our name is called.”

OSU entered the fourth quarter up 56-27, and while the Cowgirls did have a comfortable lead, there was some disappointment in the building.

Making only 39% from the field, the offensive efficiency was not what OSU had grown accustomed to. However, the Cowgirls quickly flipped a switch in the fourth.

OSU brought new energy to its offense, marking a field goal percentage of 63%. The Cowgirls also totaled 36 points in the fourth quarter, greater than its entire first-half output of 34 points.

The final quarter gave OSU fans and the team a good taste to end on before conference play begins Saturday against Iowa State.

“Everybody is going to bring their best,” Jones said. “And when the coaches can expect that, and it happens every night, it's just a blessing to be a part of a team like this because this is rare. Very, very rare.”
