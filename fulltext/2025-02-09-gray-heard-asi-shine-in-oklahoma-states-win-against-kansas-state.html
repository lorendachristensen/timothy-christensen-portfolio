Gray, Heard, Asi shine in Oklahoma State's win against Kansas State

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2025-02-09
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Coming off a loss to West Virginia, No. 25 Oklahoma State utilized its bye week to prepare for a top-25 matchup against No. 12 Kansas State. The 85-55 blow-out was the largest margin of victory by the Cowgirls against a ranked opponent.

Here's OSU's players of the game against K-State.

Despite facing a top-12 team in the country, the Cowgirls never let the game be that close. A hot start to the first quarter was generated by Gray, who put up OSU’s first points with a three-pointer that got the record-attendance crowd loud and rowdy.

Gray gave OSU an early lead by scoring seven of the Cowgirls' first 11 points. With Gray leading the way, OSU got out to a 31-9 lead after a quarter.

Gray’s defensive performance helped the Cowgirls, too, and she collected five defensive rebounds.

Heard scored only seven points in OSU's loss to WVU. But on Saturday, she was able to bounce back.

Heard dropped 21 points and led the team with 11 defensive rebounds while dishing five assists.

“I think it's [Heard’s] stability and consistency that's kind of given everyone else confidence and helping us be consistent," Cowgirls head coach Jacie Hoyt said.

Asi, the senior from Estonia, led OSU with 24 points drilled eight shots from beyond the arc. Asi also led the team with 6 assists

Asi caught fire quickly, starting the game eight of nine from deep. The 3-pointers gave the Gallagher Iba Arena crowd something to cheer about.

While others got to get in extra reps during the buy week, Asi did not get those same benefits. During the week, Asi, among other players, contracted the flu and was forced to miss practice.

“It wasn't the recovery we thought that we would get”, Coach Hoyt stated. “But I am so incredibly proud of the maturity that our kids had on really just a two-day prep, not even with our whole team, to follow the game plan the way that we asked them to.”

Overall, the performances from all of the Cowgirl players were what they needed to get the season back on track to stay in the Big 12 race.

“We needed to remind ourselves who we are and just come out with that reckless, abandoned mindset”, Hoyt said. “And we certainly did that...We made the statement we wanted to today”
