Oklahoma State defeats Arizona 83-64, improves to 20-4

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2025-02-13
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Oklahoma State was able to defend its home arena with an 83-64 victory against the Arizona Wildcats.

Following their upset win against Kansas State, the Cowgirls needed to avoid a letdown game against a frisky Arizona team, which has leaned on its defense that gave up only 62 points per game before Wednesday's game.

The Cowgirls committed uncharacteristic fouls and made uncommon mistakes in the first quarter. They had three early fouls and five turnovers in the beginning quarter alone. OSU also had trouble from beyond the arc, making only 1 of 9 3-point shots.

Despite the start, the Wildcats were never able to capitalize on the mistakes made by the Cowgirls. The Cowgirls garnered 13 second-chance points in the first quarter, which helped them keep the game tied at 16-16 at the end of the sloppy quarter.

Arizona’s star player Jada Williams suffered a leg injury at the end of the quarter, limiting her playing time to 7 minutes. Head coach Jacie Hoyt and OSU were able to take advantage of the lack of depth on the young Wildcats team.

OSU started living in the paint and attempting to draw fouls. By the end of the first half, the Cowgirls were up 37-29. 30 of those first-half points for OSU were either gained in the paint or from free throws.

The floodgates opened in the second half for OSU offensively. The Cowgirls continued attacking the rim but were also able to start making shots from the three as well. A big component of that was leading scorer Micah Gray. She went 3-of-3 from beyond the arc to go along with 20 points.

“We're just taking it one game at a time. We've learned the hard way what can happen when we look ahead,” Jacie Hoyt stated. “Now that we've positioned ourselves where we have, it’s about just understanding how important every single game is, and in getting better every game, because we still can get so much better as a team.”
