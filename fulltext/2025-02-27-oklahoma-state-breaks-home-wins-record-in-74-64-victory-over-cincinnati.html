Oklahoma State breaks home wins record in 74-64 victory over Cincinnati

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2025-02-27
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Oklahoma State finished all its regular-season home games with a 17-1 home record, defeating the Cincinnati Bearcats 74-64.

The Cowgirls were able to take control from opening tip-off, never trailing the Bearcats. The opening points of the game came from two free throws senior Alexia Smith with 8:57 left in the first quarter.

The strong start continued for OSU thanks to some stingy defense in the first half. The Bearcats were only able to make 24% of their shots from the field and 16% from beyond the arc. OSU was also able to limit Bearcats' star player Jillian Hayes to 6 points in the opening half. The fast start helped the Cowgirls go to the locker room up 38-21.

The second half wasn't as pretty for the Cowgirls.

The first-half lead helped the Cowgirls through a sloppy second half, where the Bearcats outscored OSU 43-36. The once disciplined defense turned into an aggressive one. The fourth quarter saw three Cowgirl players foul out, including Macey Huard and both Cowgirl centers Tenin Magassa and Praise Egharevba.

The Bearcats started showing signs of life in the second half, going on a 9-1 run and a 10-2 run. The comeback never got Cincinnati within striking range, but it was enough to not let OSU or its fans feel comfortable.

However, a win is a win, and this was a big one. This victory clinched a top-4 seed for the Cowgirls in the Big 12 tournament and the highly coveted double-bye in the bracket.

The win also marked the highest home-win total in team history, at 17. The win total sparked record-breaking attendance during the 2024-25 season, and OSU coach Jacie Hoyt explained how the fans have helped the team this season.

“When [the fans are] in the stands and you go on a run, make a big shot and they go crazy, that's something that gives us energy, and we feed off of that” Hoyt said. “I think our student body has supported us by far the most this season of any of my years here. So I hope we can keep building it because they make a huge difference.”

Hoyt and the Cowgirls will hope to get payback for their lone home loss when they head to Kansas for the regular season finale on Sunday.
