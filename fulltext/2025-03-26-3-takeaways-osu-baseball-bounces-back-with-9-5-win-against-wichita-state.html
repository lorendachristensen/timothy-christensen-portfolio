3 takeaways: OSU baseball bounces back with 9-5 win against Wichita State

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2025-03-26
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

After a rocky weekend, Oklahoma State found its way back into the win column Tuesday night with a 9-5 win against Wichita State at O’Brate Stadium.

Here are three takeaways from The Cowboys' midweek win.

OSU was down 1-0 to start the fourth inning. Wanting to keep the deficit thin, pitcher Mario Pesca had a tough task when he entered the field with bases loaded and no outs.

WSU’s Snyder Gannon hit a ground ball right into the infield that aided the Cowboys in a double play. Two batters and a ground ball later, there were three outs, and WSU was left with only a single point after loading the bases.

“You’ve got to stay pretty uniform upstairs,” Pesca said. “You’ve got to call the pitch. I know I could do that pretty well… and don't try to build it up or make it smaller, and just go in as it is and try the same mind frame, regardless.”

Pesca pitched two shutout innings and didn't allow a hit while picking up the win.

Fourth-inning offensive explosion helps Cowboys take control

After being held scoreless during the first three innings, OSU knew that it needed to get its bats going.

And the Cowboys opened up the flood gates in the fourth.

After a couple of single hits and a walk from his teammates, Jayson Jones came up to the plate with bases loaded.

Ball, ball, ball, ball: OSU scores its first point of the game.

Then DH Colin Brueggemann also got to step into the batter's box for a bases-loaded situation, but he ended up having a full count.

On the sixth try of the at-bat, Bruggemann slammed the ball into the outfield wall, which resulted in a triple and three more runs for the Cowboys.

OSU's victory was a morale booster after the tough weekend the team had in Salt Lake City. Pitching and hitting struggles reared its head over the Utah series and was something that the team wanted to fix coming back home.

The pitching improved slightly, with some good stretches from Mathew Brown and Pesca. But the real improvement in Tuesday's game was at the plate.

While it took a while for OSU to get the bats going, the fourth inning opened up the game. After the fourth inning, Brayden Smith and Nolan Schubart hit back-to-back home runs in the fifth. Two scores from Kollin Ritchie, including stealing home, got the Cowboys to nine runs.

“Try not to do too much,” Smith stated, “That's kind of a common theme. Just relax and put the ball on the barrel. Don't make it more than it is… Just hit the ball hard.”
