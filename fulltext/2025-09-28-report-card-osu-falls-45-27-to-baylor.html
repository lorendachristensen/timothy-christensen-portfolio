Report card: OSU falls 45-27 to Baylor

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2025-09-28
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

In the first game in the post-Gundy era, the Oklahoma State Cowboys fell to the Baylor Bears 45-27, dropping OSU to 1-3 on the season and extending its Big 12 losing streak to 10 straight games over the last two seasons.

Here’s a report card for the game’s key contributors.

In his first attempt as head coach, Meacham was able to find his footing, replacing the big shoes of Mike Gundy.

While the best-case scenario of a win evaded the rookie head coach, he was able to do the next best thing: avoid big mistakes.

That is not to say that Meacham didn’t get to leave at least some of his fingerprints on the game. Early game trick plays and aggressive play calling sent a message that Meacham is not ready to throw away the season.

Despite only being named coach in the middle of the week, Meachum was able to set a template for the team for the rest of his tenure, however long that lasts.

Despite the Cowboys' offense scoring the most points it had all season, the team never had a lead because the Baylor offense was able to move up and down the field.

The season-long issue of tackling continued again on Saturday, leading to the Bears gaining 219 yards on the ground and 393 yards through the air.

The defensive performance also signifies a troubling pattern for the team. Including the loss to Oregon 69-3, OSU has given up a total of 1,243 yards and 114 points to the only two Power Four opponents they have faced.

With the firing of Mike Gundy and the early-season struggles, there was doubt about how much interest there would be from fans about this year’s team.

Despite the turmoil, Cowboys fans still came out to the first home Saturday game of the season to support the team and the new coach. Early in the matchup, there was lots of energy in the building. The student section cheered on all the triumphs and yelled for every Bear third down.

Throughout the game, as the heat started to take a toll and the game started to get out of hand, the energy and fans slowly left the building. However, OSU athletics has to be pleased with the results, taking all context into consideration.
