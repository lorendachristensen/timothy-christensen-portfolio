Report card: OSU falls 42-0 to Texas Tech

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2025-10-27
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Oklahoma State was unable to right the ship to the disappointing season when it traveled down to Lubbock to take on No. 14 Texas Tech, losing 42-0.

Here’s a report card for the Cowboys in the Big 12 matchup:

While it might be unfair, the special team’s coverage units do not want to be mentioned in a game because it usually signals that disaster has struck. OSU’s special teams were the center of attention early in the game.

To start the game, Texas Tech running back J’Koby Williams was able to bring the opening kickoff 99 yards for the first touchdown of the game. Williams’ play showcased a common phenomenon against the Cowboys: missed tackles.

After a three-and-out from the OSU offense and with 13:07 left in the opening quarter, Wes Paul’s punt was blocked, setting the Texas Tech offense up with great field position, which led to six points the very next play.

The early game mistakes put the Cowboys in a hole, from which they were unable to make their way out.

Sam Jackson V struggled in his third straight start for the Cowboys, going 9-of-19 passing and accumulating only 48 yards.

Noah Walters didn’t fare much better in his playing time during the fourth quarter, going 5 of 12 for 39 passing yards.

Perhaps a more apt grade for this QB room would be an incomplete, with both Hauss Hejny and Zane Flores out after suffering injuries earlier in the season. Without those quarterbacks, the OSU offense has been stagnant with the exception of some exciting trick plays sprinkled in. However, today was the offense’s first time being shut out.

One of the few bright spots for the Cowboys was their ability to limit the run, something that has been a tough feat throughout much of the season.

Coming into the day, Texas Tech was averaging 215 rushing yards a game, led sophomore Cameron Dickey.

However, the OSU defense was able to hold Texas Tech to only 88 rushing yards and a scarce 2 yards per carry.

The Red Raiders’ success in throwing the ball and scoring 42 points shows that extra rushing yards were not necessary. However, OSU will surely want to build on the progress made in stopping the run.
