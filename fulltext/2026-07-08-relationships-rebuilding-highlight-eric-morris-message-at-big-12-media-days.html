Relationships, rebuilding highlight Eric Morris' message at Big 12 Media Days

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-07-08
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

FRISCO, Texas — As football season inches closer, the Big 12 held its annual Media Days at The Star in Frisco, Texas, on Tuesday, giving coaches and players an opportunity to discuss the upcoming season.

Eric Morris was among the first coaches to meet with reporters, where he touched on familiar topics, including praising Oklahoma State leadership, such as President Jim Hess and athletic director Chad Weiberg, along with former North Texas players Drew Mestemaker and Caleb Hawkins. Here are some of the more notable takeaways from Morris' session.

"I've been in the Big 12 as a player and a coach, and Oklahoma State was so good in my memories," Morris said. "... Now, when I got there, I had to sit back and realize that there have been kids that have been there for two years that haven't experienced walking in that locker room after winning a conference game."

Morris' observation shows respect for the new organization he has joined while also acknowledging that he has a lot of work to do to rebuild it into contention. Statements like this will inevitably raise the standard and expectation for this next era of Cowboy football.

In the short term, however, simply earning a conference victory would be progress after last season's winless Big 12 campaign, and Morris appears focused on laying a foundation for long-term success.

"From our very first team meeting, all the North Texas guys were sitting in the front right, and I made everybody stand up and go sit by somebody they didn't know, so we could immediately start forming new relationships," Morris said.

Morris spent much of his media session emphasizing the importance of relationships between coaches and players and among teammates. He believes meaningful connections are essential to building a successful football program.

Defensive lineman Jaleel Johnson remembered that first meeting well.

"Everybody hesitated to move," Johnson said. "Nobody really wanted to do it, but we knew we needed to, and we ended up doing it. It started the beautiful brotherhood."

Morris has organized team-building activities ranging from breakfasts and wiffle ball to golf outings and pool gatherings at his home. Early on, those efforts appear to have strengthened the bonds within the program, though those relationships will ultimately be tested during the season.

Another topic that dominated Tuesday's discussions was the future of the College Football Playoff.

"I think that's what everybody in America wants to see," Morris said. "... They want to see the best against the best for as many weeks as possible, right?"

All 16 Big 12 head coaches indicated they support expanding the playoff to 24 teams.

A larger playoff would also give conferences like the Big 12 a better opportunity to place multiple teams in the field. Last season, only Texas Tech represented the conference in the playoff.

Though Big 12 Commissioner Brett Yormark supports an expanded playoff bracket, he also acknowledged that the league is still working to mitigate any "unintended consequences" that expansion might bring. The league's largest issue is the potential elimination of the conference championship game and the revenue it generates to make room for extra playoff games in the schedule.

Despite the uncertainty surrounding college football's future, Morris said his focus remains on building unity within his team.

"We have so many different people that come from different backgrounds, races, socioeconomic classes, religions… we are all more alike than we ever imagined," Morris said. "... It's not about us and them. This is all about Oklahoma State versus a really good conference."
