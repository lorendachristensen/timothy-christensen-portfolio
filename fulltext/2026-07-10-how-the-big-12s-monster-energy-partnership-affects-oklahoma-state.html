How the Big 12's Monster Energy partnership affects Oklahoma State

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-07-10
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

The days of logo-free college uniforms are over in the Big 12.

Oklahoma State and the conference have each announced new sponsorship agreements that will place corporate logos on uniforms beginning with the 2026-27 athletic season.

The first brand logo will be that of the Osage Nation, owners of the Osage Casino Hotel near the OSU-Tulsa campus. The partnership was announced June 23 in an OSU Athletics press release.

"Osage is proud to be able to support the student athletes and programs across the athletic department," said Kimberly Pearson, CEO of Osage Casinos and Hotels. "We've built a strong connection with the university through other projects, and this brings the relationship to a broader stage. It's about visibility, opportunity and continuing to invest in the future of our people and our communities."

The move is unprecedented in OSU history, making Osage the first sponsor mark on an OSU varsity uniform. The timing is no coincidence. As college athletics searches for new revenue in the NIL and revenue-sharing era, sponsorships have become another avenue for schools and conferences to generate revenue. In the NIL era, OSU has explored multiple avenues to generate additional revenue, including the Boys from Oklahoma concerts and its proposed helmet QR code initiative in 2024.

"It is an early confirmation of what is possible with our department's new organizational restructure to generate new revenue growth and commercial activity," said athletic director Chad Weiberg.

Just weeks later, the Big 12 took another step into the commercial landscape and announced at Tuesday's Media Day that it was entering into an entitlement agreement with energy drink Monster.

The agreement places Monster branding on conference-controlled football and basketball uniforms, championship events and other conference assets, making the Big 12 the first major conference to announce a league-wide naming-rights partnership of its kind.

"Big 12 football, men's and women's basketball will be branded as Monster Energy Big 12 football and Monster Energy Big 12 basketball for all conference-controlled assets and platforms," said Big 12 Commissioner Brett Yormark. "A co-branded Monster Energy and Big 12 Conference logo will be featured on football and basketball jerseys, fields and courts, with additional integration across conference, digital and social media channels."

Front Office Sports reports that the deal includes $20 million, with about $1 million being offered to each Big 12 school.

As college athletics continues to adapt to the NIL and revenue-sharing era, sponsorship agreements such as those with the Osage Nation and Monster Energy illustrate how schools and conferences are seeking new ways to generate revenue while keeping pace in an increasingly expensive landscape.
