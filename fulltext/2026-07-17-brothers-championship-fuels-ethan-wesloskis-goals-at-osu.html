Brother's championship fuels Ethan Wesloski's goals at OSU

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-07-17
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

There is nothing quite like a healthy sibling rivalry to light a competitive fire under players.

Linebacker Ethan Wesloski was one of the 17 players who followed head coach Eric Morris from North Texas to Oklahoma State. He had a breakout year with UNT last season, playing in all 14 games for the Mean Green and totaling 113 tackles, including 59 solo tackles. His efforts earned him a spot on the All-American Conference Second Team.

While most of OSU’s offseason was highlighted by offensive additions, Ethan, a senior, brings talent and experience to a defense that has struggled heavily the past two seasons.

Despite his success, Ethan is still striving for more, and the bar has been set by his little brother, Nick Wesloski.

Nick was a freshman pitcher for the Oklahoma baseball team in 2025, contributing to the Sooners’ improbable run to the national championship.

Despite finishing the regular season as the SEC’s 11th seed, the Sooners caught fire in the Men’s College World Series, defeating teams including No. 2 Georgia Tech, No. 3 Georgia and No. 5 North Carolina.

Nick made his only three starts during his freshman year in the postseason, two of which were in the MCWS. He started for the Sooners’ 13-2 win in the MCWS Final Game 3 against the UNC Tar Heels, striking out three while allowing five hits and one run over 2 1/3 innings.

That sibling rivalry has only intensified now that Ethan wears orange and Nick wears crimson. With the brothers now on opposite sides of Bedlam, the competition carries even more meaning.

Ethan was blunt about what he needs to do this season to keep up with Nick’s trophy case:

“I've got to win it,” Ethan said. “I've got to win the national championship… I’m going to try my hardest. I am going to go out there every day and compete. I’m going to try and win the national championship game.”

Morris believes experienced players like Ethan are exactly what OSU needed during its rebuild, stating that he is “really hoping that that experience pays off for us in year one.”

Along with his leadership, Ethan believes experience gives him another advantage.

"I've been able to understand schematics," Ethan said. "I am in my fourth year starting, but it'll be my fifth year in college football. I've been around a lot of football; I understand what offenses like to go to in distances and situations."

Matching his younger brother's championship won't be easy after the Cowboys' 1-11 season, but Oklahoma's title run wasn't expected either, giving Ethan confidence that anything is possible.

“There's a lot of positive stuff coming,” Ethan said. “There's a lot of exciting football coming. We're gonna cause a lot of chaos on defense, and this offense is electric, as you know. There's a lot of games coming that we're gonna win, and it's going to be exciting to watch.”
