‘That’s home’: Jaleel Johnson discusses culture shift in his fifth year of OSU football

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-07-31
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Defensive edge Jaleel Johnson has seen the ups and downs of Oklahoma State football.

Following his redshirt season in 2022, in which he did not see any in-game action, Johnson played in 12 games for the Cowboys during his freshman season in 2023. The season resulted in a 10-4 record, a Big 12 Championship appearance and a victory in the final Bedlam game.

The 2024 season became a shocking turning point for the Mike Gundy era, with the team posting a 3-9 record and finishing winless in Big 12 play. The following season was even worse, with Johnson playing only four games due to injury as the team went 1-11.

Despite the past two seasons being filled with poor play, plummeting expectations and unprecedented turnover on the roster and coaching staff, Johnson still decided to play his fifth year at OSU. At Big 12 Media Days earlier this month, he talked about why he believes he still needed to be a Cowboy under head coach Eric Morris’ new leadership.

“The biggest reason I stayed was that when Morris got the job, he made an effort to call me,” Johnson said. “He told me that he wanted me to be here. He wanted me to play here. He wanted me to help turn the ship around. So, it was kind of an easy decision.”

Morris selected Johnson to be one of the four OSU players to attend the media days, despite Johnson’s reluctance.

“He obviously had a little bit of anxiety speaking in front of people,” Morris said. “He has a great personality, and it's been a lot of fun getting to know him… He’s been [at OSU] his whole career. There’s not a better person to come and represent this university that he’s given so much to.”

Morris has not been shy about praising the senior, describing Johnson as “the most disruptive player” during spring practice and saying he is expecting a breakout season from the defensive lineman.

In a locker room full of transfers, Johnson is one of the few players left from the old Gundy regime. Johnson shares the same perspective as many OSU fans. He was there for the field-storming following Bedlam in the Cowboys’ 27-24 victory over Oklahoma in 2023 and witnessed the shirtless fans in section 231.

Johnson has balanced respect for the past with the changes needed for the future.

Despite the poor results from the past two seasons, Johnson experienced what it takes to win in 2023, and he believes that experience can help this team.

“The guys coming in from North Texas are younger,” Johnson said. “I've been in this game longer than them; I feel like I still have things that I can help them with. And they have things that can help me with, because they came from a winning culture.”

Spending an entire career at one school is becoming increasingly rare in college football, but heading into his fifth season, Johnson is happy with his decision to stay in Stillwater.

“I'm big on loyalty, man,” Johnson said. “Knowing that if there ever is a reunion, I don't have to worry about, ‘Oh, which school should I go to?’ No, I know I can come back to Oklahoma State because that's home. That's home.”
