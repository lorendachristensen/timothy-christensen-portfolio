Backup quarterback battle takes shape at OSU fall camp

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-08-06
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

While Drew Mestemaker is firmly entrenched as Oklahoma State's starting quarterback, head coach Eric Morris and his staff still have decisions to make behind him on the depth chart.

OSU quarterbacks Grant Jordan and Broderick "Brodie" Vehrs are currently competing to be the backup quarterback this season. While being the backup QB is not the most glamorous title, Morris understands the position’s importance.

“I think that's something that's probably understated here,” Morris said. “Obviously, what Oklahoma State went through last year, you get excited about a kid that starts good in series two or three, he's out for the rest of the year, and it's like holy crap. What do we do now?”

In 2025, Hauss Hejny won the starting job for the season opener against UT Martin, with plans to split time with Zane Flores. Hejny accounted for two touchdowns — one passing and one rushing — in his lone quarter of action.

That quarter ended up being Hejny's only action of the season after he suffered a broken foot. Four games later, Flores was injured, and the Cowboys turned to former receiver Sam Jackson V to quarterback the team. Despite Flores' eventual return, the Cowboys never won a game after Hejny's injury.

Morris hopes to avoid a similar situation this season, with Jordan and Vehrs emerging as the top candidates for the backup quarterback job.

The current frontrunner seems to be Jordan. The redshirt senior transferred to OSU after spending time at Yale and UMass.

After missing time due to injury during his first couple of seasons at Yale, Jordan started in nine games for the Bulldogs, passing for 1,938 yards and 22 touchdowns with a 66% completion rate. He then transferred to UMass and was mostly the backup, starting in only two games.

Now that he is at OSU, Morris appreciates Jordan’s football experience.

“Grant is a seasoned veteran,” Morris said. “He's played football. He's been around. He's installed. Super smart. Continues to get better.”

Vehrs comes to OSU as a true freshman from Gilbert, Arizona, out of Basha High School. He was ranked the No. 65 quarterback prospect in the national class and the No. 15 overall prospect in the state of Arizona, according to Rivals.

Jordan enters camp with a significant experience advantage, while Vehrs brings the higher long-term upside as one of the program's most highly regarded freshman signees.

Despite Jordan’s experience edge over the freshman, Morris is not willing to call the QB competition over at this point in the offseason.

“Brodie's a guy I think that's right on [Jordan’s] tail right now,” Morris said. “He’s a young guy that got all those reps in the spring. I think we'll give them a ton of reps in live scrimmage situations to see who can kind of put it all together.”

Morris and OSU fans hope the QB2 battle never matters this season, but after injuries forced the Cowboys to cycle through multiple quarterbacks in 2025, identifying a dependable backup has become one of fall camp's biggest priorities.
