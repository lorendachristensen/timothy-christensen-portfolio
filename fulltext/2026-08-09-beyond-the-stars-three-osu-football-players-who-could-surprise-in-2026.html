Beyond the stars: Three OSU football players who could surprise in 2026

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-08-09
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

With 87 newcomers, there are a lot of unfamiliar names on the 2026 Oklahoma State football roster.

Head coach Eric Morris placed heavy emphasis on bringing in fresh blood to Stillwater this season, a fitting approach to steer the program away from the 1-11 campaign in 2025.

Morris brought over some big names with him from North Texas, including quarterback Drew Mestemaker, running back Caleb Hawkins, wide receiver Wyatt Young and linebacker Ethan Wesloski. Those players' previous production makes them obvious stars to watch this season.

Morris and his staff helped those players emerge nationally last year at North Texas, and he will attempt to find other hidden gems at OSU. As the team begins fall camp, a few early candidates could surprise fans this year.

Here are a couple of dark-horse players to keep an eye on this season:

Bodnar transferred to OSU from Liberty for his junior year. Bodnar played all over the secondary for the Flames, playing some corner and safety. Morris plans to utilize Bodnar at nickelback this season.

Bodnar was one of three players to win the 2025 Comeback Player of the Year Award after missing most of 2024 with a rare condition that caused him to lose the ability to walk temporarily.

Now that he has teamed up with Morris and defensive coordinator Skyler Cassity, there is a lot of enthusiasm around the impact Bodnar can make on this OSU defense.

“I think he’s a guy that is gonna be really, really a household name by the end of this thing,” Morris said. “How well he can play man coverage in the slot. He’s a great blitzer off the edge. We can be aggressive with him. He tackles well in open space. Super-excited.”

Bowick has transferred several times during his college career, spending time at Eastern Illinois, Ball State and Illinois. Now Bowick will spend his redshirt senior year at OSU.

Bowick has never truly broken out as an elite pass catcher with his former teams, but his size and length are undeniable. He stands at 6-foot-5, making him the tallest receiver on the roster.

Outside of his size, Morris also complimented Bowick’s work ethic this summer.

“Almost every time I went in [the training facility], Justin Bowick’s in there,” Morris said. “Whether he’s catching jugs, working on his craft… I think he’s an older kid, but he’s super committed to the processes of getting better, and I think you’ve really been able to see that pay off.”

With his catch radius in Morris’ offensive system, Bowick could be poised for a breakout season in Stillwater.

Johnson might be the player fans are most familiar with because he has spent his entire college career at OSU.

Despite a tumultuous tenure with the Cowboys, Johnson has stuck around for his senior year to play under Morris and Cassity.

Morris has praised Johnson all offseason, calling him the “most disruptive player” of spring practices and claiming the defensive end has the “size and talent to play on Sundays.”

Johnson is ready for football season to continue ramping up.

“I cannot wait to put pads on and really get physical with these guys and see who's really top dog,” Johnson said. “… We've only had a couple of days, but I can already tell the energy shift in the team and how we're locking in to actually get ready for this upcoming season.”
