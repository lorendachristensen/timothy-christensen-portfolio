How Skyler Cassity retooled OSU’s defensive line

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-08-13
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

There is a saying that football games are won and lost at the line of scrimmage, and Oklahoma State felt the wrong side of that in 2025.

Last year, OSU’s defensive line struggled to affect opposing offenses, allowing 166 rushing yards a game and only generating 23 sacks. In-season coordinator changes and the loss of edge rusher Jaleel Johnson to injury after four games did not help the situation either.

This year, former North Texas defensive coordinator Skyler Cassity will be calling plays for the Cowboys and believes he can provide some consistency to the OSU defense. He believes it will all start from his defensive line.

“Obviously, you want to make [an opposing] team one-dimensional,” Cassity said. “We’re trying to make teams play left-handed. That’s the best way to view it. Take away what they do best. Obviously, you've got to be dominant up front. They've got to play a gap and a half. They've got to control the line of scrimmage.”

The coaching staff prioritized size and experience along the defensive line, retaining redshirt seniors Jaleel Johnson, Malik Charles and Iman Oates.

Johnson has been described as the “most disruptive player” on the field during practices, and Cassity said he was “really excited” to get Charles back this spring after he had been nursing an injury.

Oates was a late addition to the Cowboys’ offseason after he secured a temporary restraining order allowing him to play a fifth year of college football. Head coach Eric Morris said that despite his absence from the team, Oates reported to camp in “phenomenal shape.”

Cassity expects the players’ experience to help the defense make an immediate impact.

“Having a veteran group, obviously, it gives them experience,” Cassity said. “They're not making the same mistakes. They do a good job of understanding what's going on in front of them, right, which allows them to play a little bit faster, obviously, in multiple dynamics.”

OSU secured more defensive line talent in the transfer portal, including UNT defensive tackles Saadiq Clements and Fatafehi Vailea II, as well as Louisville transfer Jerry Lawson.

Cassity is looking forward to the amount of defensive line talent he’ll get to utilize this season.

“Our rotation and ability to keep those guys fresh, where they're not playing 45 snaps a game, is one of our advantages,” Cassity said. “I mean, we have confidence in a lot of guys that can go out there and execute and execute at a high level.

"I would say it's as good as you can get. I mean, this is probably as deep as I've ever been up front, as far as bodies and guys that can go in and execute at a high level. This is the deepest I've ever been anywhere I've coached, so we feel confident in that group."

If the hype is real about this defensive line, then the Cowboys might have turned one of their biggest weaknesses into one of their biggest strengths all in one offseason.

Cassity quoted Seattle Seahawks’ head coach Mike Macdonald when describing what an elite defensive line could bring to this football team.

“The moment that the four defensive linemen can defeat the five offensive linemen, good things happen for defenses,” Cassity said.
