Svoboda emphasizing versatility as OSU tight end room takes shape

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-08-16
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Despite the talent in the room, Oklahoma State tight ends coach Drew Svoboda believes there is still plenty of room for the group to grow.

The role of a tight end involves both blocking and catching, and an elite tight end can perform both at a high level.

When revamping the tight end room, head coach Eric Morris and his staff sought players who could fill all the roles the position needs, resulting in a diverse tight end group.

While the group features a variety of skill sets, Svoboda wants each player to become more well-rounded.

"They do have a diversity of skill set, but they have to have some common characteristics that they all have to be good at,” Svoboda said. “They are going to have strengths and weaknesses, but it can't be so drastic that when we put this guy in we're running this play or when we put this guy in we're running that play. That is a red flag, obviously, to a defensive coordinator.”

With multiple tight ends bringing different strengths to the room, Svoboda wants to make sure no player becomes predictable based on who is on the field.

Redshirt senior Oscar Hammonds seems to be buying into that philosophy.

Hammonds followed Morris and Svoboda from North Texas to Stillwater. He missed all of last season for the Mean Green due to injury, but appears to be healthy and ready to play for the Cowboys this year.

Hammonds spent two years at North Texas and looks to provide the versatility that Svoboda wants from the tight end position.

“He's got strong hands and great ball skills, and he has really been in this system multiple years because of the time he spent with us down in Denton,” Svoboda said. “He is strong and he is big and can block. He can do all the things necessary. He is probably ahead of all of (the tight ends) with regard to his knowledge of the scheme. It's as simple as the signals and recognizing what we call this and what we call that."

Redshirt senior Donovan Green also has earned praise during fall camp.

“Last week was a great week for him,” Svoboda said. “He definitely was a better version of himself than we saw in the spring. He has to continue to come along, and he's played a lot of football. He has a lot of experience. He's got to continue to just keep chipping away at it."

Green transferred to OSU after serving as a backup at LSU last season. As a former four-star prospect out of high school, Green has the physical tools to make an impact for this Cowboy offense.

As the tight end group takes shape during fall camp, Svoboda continues to emphasize the importance of versatility from the position.

“Are they great at everything? No,” Svoboda said. “There are going to be some things that they are better at than others. (It’s about) having the mental approach that they are good at just about everything and not being so extreme on the spectrum."
