The Turnpike Classic: How OSU vs. Tulsa has become a real rivalry

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-09-01
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Oklahoma State football’s trip to Tulsa this week has more meaning than it ever has before.

The “Turnpike Classic” between the Cowboys and the Golden Hurricane has never received the attention that other in-state rivalries garner, despite the two organizations competing 77 times over the last 111 years. However, OSU fans have had their eye on another for most of that history.

For years, the University of Oklahoma was circled by everyone involved at OSU athletics. Fans would always tune in to Bedlam football, regardless of either team's record.

As OU transitioned to the SEC, the Cowboys and Golden Hurricane found it mutually beneficial to ramp up their own rivalry, eventually leading to contracts in place for the teams to play from 2019 to 2031.

Despite the Turnpike Classic becoming the only Division I football rivalry left in the state, the game lacked appeal among general fanbases. TU had not beaten OSU since 1998, leading some Cowboy fans to look past the Golden Hurricane each year.

TU head coach Tre Lamb took notice.

On Sept. 19, 2025, TU stunned Boone Pickens Stadium, beating OSU 19-12. The loss ended any optimism that the Cowboys would rebound from their lackluster 2024 season, and OSU decided to fire the program’s head coach of 20 years, Mike Gundy.

After the game, Lamb was adamant about how much the win meant for him in his first season as head coach.

“That's a huge win for our program,” Lamb said. “It's a statement win for me and our staff and our administration. I told the team, ‘New Tulsa's back,’ and I told the ESPN guys, ‘We don't want to be on the second page of the paper.’ … I can't tell you how big this is for us in this state, being the little brother, and we play with a chip on our shoulder.”

Despite TU finishing 4-8 in 2025, the statement win provided hope for a Lamb-led rebuild in Tulsa.

For OSU, the loss marked a turning point. After finishing the year without Gundy, the Cowboys turned toward Eric Morris to lead them into the next era of Cowboy football. The team experienced significant turnover during the offseason.

While the Cowboys likely already had increased hostility toward his team, Lamb added fuel to the fire over the offseason.

When hyping up the crowd at a TU basketball game in February, Lamb vehemently declared, “We’ll beat their ass again,” in reference to the orange and black. The risky quote is perfect material to create bad blood between the two teams.

OSU defensive lineman Jaleel Johnson was in Stillwater for the loss last season. He was asked on Tuesday whether the team heard Lamb’s statements.

“We heard, we heard,” Johnson said with a smile on his face. “We ain't gonna say too much, but we definitely took notice of it.”

Lamb’s bravado does not come without respect, however, and he spoke highly of the new-look Cowboys.

“This is the most talented team we’re gonna play,” Lamb told News on 6 on Aug. 26. “That’s just what I told the guys. I do feel that way. I think they have a first-round quarterback. I think Coach Morris is a really good evaluator of talent, him and his staff, Raj [Murti], their GM. They’ve done an unreal job of bringing good players in, and we know what’s in front of us.”

Lamb might be trying to bring down the temperature now, but players like Johnson already see this upcoming game as “personal.” Whether it be history, proximity or vengeance, there certainly will be an added weight to this year’s Turnpike Classic.
