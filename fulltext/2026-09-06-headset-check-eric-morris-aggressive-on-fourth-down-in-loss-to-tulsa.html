Headset Check: Eric Morris aggressive on fourth down in loss to Tulsa

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-09-06
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

TULSA — Oklahoma State had to resort to being the more desperate team in Saturday’s 24-10 loss to the Tulsa Golden Hurricane.

Eric Morris made his head coaching debut for the Cowboys, and it came with less-than-stellar results. TU controlled the majority of the matchup with disciplined offense and disruptive defense, never allowing OSU to find its footing.

While playing from behind, Morris made aggressive fourth down decisions. OSU went for it on fourth down six times and was only successful twice.

The Cowboys attempted two fourth downs on their opening drive, handing the ball up the middle to running back Caleb Hawkins on both. While Hawkins picked up the first down on his first try, OSU ultimately came away with no points after he was stuffed at the 3-yard line.

The Cowboys also went for a fourth-and-1 with 7:31 left in the second quarter. Running back Tre Page III passed the first-down marker before fumbling on the same play, giving TU the ball on their own 32.

However, not all the fourth-down attempts were disastrous.

Late in the third quarter, receiver Justin Bowick caught the Cowboys’ only touchdown on fourth-and-7. The play was a rare bright spot for the OSU fans in attendance in Tulsa.

OSU did not convert any of its last four fourth-and-1 situations, with most of the plays just being runs up the middle. Even if Morris stands by his aggressiveness, he understands the results were not what he had hoped for.

“That's where for me and I got to go back and look schematically,” Morris said. “‘Is it something that we get Drew out of the pocket? And do we do something to utilize his arm more in those situations?’ But I thought [Tulsa] did a nice job at being able to take away gaps and put some pressure on us… I just told the kids in there, those are on me. I'm the play caller, and I didn't think the scheme was where it needs to be. Got out-coached in those situations.”
