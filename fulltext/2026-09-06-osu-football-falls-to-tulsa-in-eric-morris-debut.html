OSU football falls to Tulsa in Eric Morris' debut

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-09-06
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

TULSA — Despite the Oklahoma State Cowboys’ transformative offseason, the Tulsa Golden Hurricane reigned victorious for the second straight season, winning 24-10.

The Turnpike Classic delivered similar results to last season, with offensive disappointment and confusion plaguing OSU (0-1) throughout the game.

Much of the game was slow for the Cowboys’ offense. Sloppy plays and throws from quarterback Drew Mestemaker prevented the group from finding any type of rhythm.

A promising opening drive for the Cowboys was halted on the 3-yard line, after running back Caleb Hawkins was stuffed at the line of scrimmage on fourth-and-1. The drive also resulted in a gruesome right leg injury for offensive lineman Jacob Sexton. Head coach Eric Morris spoke on what that first drive meant for his team.

“We never were able to capture any kind of momentum in this game,” Morris said. “I thought the first drive was a great representation of that. I think we drive down the field and get it down there, and they were more physical than us on the goal line.”

OSU’s first points of the season were not until the 2:04 mark in the second quarter. Those points came from the leg of Sam Keltner as he made a 35-yard field goal. Hawkins led the drive with a pair of 13- and 14-yard runs.

TU (1-0) struck back the following drive. Quarterback Baylor Hayes found receiver Oran Singleton deep downfield. Singleton stole the ball out of the air from cornerback LaDainian Fields, resulting in a 47-yard gain and first-and-goal for the Golden Hurricane.

The Cowboy defense seemed to have held the Golden Hurricane to a fourth down until a roughing the passer penalty was called on OSU’s Jaleel Johnson, giving TU a new set of downs. TU took advantage, with a direct snap to running back Damari Alston for a touchdown.

The Cowboy offense scored its only touchdown after a gutsy fourth-down call from Morris that resulted in a 16-yard touchdown catch from Justin Bowick.

OSU marched to TU’s 24-yard line with 4:21 left to go in the game, but any hopes for a comeback vanished as Mestemaker threw an interception in the back corner of the end zone to TU defensive back Zach Williams.

The loss could have been worse for the Cowboys, with TU missing two field goals after OSU turnovers.

The game marks OSU's 12th straight loss in FBS games. With UMass winning earlier in the week, the Cowboys now have the longest active FBS losing streak in college football. The stat is a harsh reality check for a team with high expectations.

Morris expressed frustration over losing an in-state rivalry.

“I think momentum is everything in sports,” Morris said. “We get on the road and come down to an in-state school. That on paper we got more resources, we got more history, we got all this stuff, and that's why they played the games, right? So, for a second year in a row, they reign supreme on us right now on the football field, and that's a fact.”

The Cowboys’ fortune does not get any easier, as they prepare to host No. 2 Oregon at Boone Pickens Stadium next week.

“So many areas to clean up,” Morris said. “Like I could sit here all night and talk about how disappointed I am for our fan base, for our administration, for all the people that put so much into this. And we got a lot of problems to fix… [Oregon is] coming into town next week. And if they watch that film, they'll be licking their chops.”
