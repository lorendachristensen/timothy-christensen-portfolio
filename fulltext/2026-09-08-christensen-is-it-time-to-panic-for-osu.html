Christensen: Is it time to panic for OSU?

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-09-08
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

It is hard to imagine a worse debut for the new-look Oklahoma State football team than Saturday's game.

Many OSU fans traveled an hour east and braced for the triple-digit heat in H.A. Chapman Stadium to see the Cowboys take on the Tulsa Golden Hurricane. The away crowd turnout was highly unusual for a team that finished 1-11 last season, but an offseason full of change and optimism completely changed how people viewed OSU.

Unfortunately for the Cowboys, that "new car smell" wore off quickly after TU controlled the season opener and beat OSU 24-10. The loss extended OSU's FBS losing streak to 21 games, the longest active streak.

Are the Cowboys destined to repeat the disappointment of the last two seasons, or will Morris' crew rebound from the loss against Tulsa?

Here are reasons to believe in each:

Lots of statements were made about this 2026 team inside and outside the locker room.

Quarterback Drew Mestemaker talked at Big 12 Media Days about his mindset of "winning wherever we go," and Morris said he wanted his team to "believe we can go out there and dominate."

What was once confidence can quickly look like arrogance if the team doesn't start winning football games. Morris understands the new situation his team is in compared to the offseason.

"There's no way that we don't leave this thing right now and feel all that momentum coming to a screeching halt," Morris said postgame at Tulsa. "It's really easy to lead when things are going well. It's hard to lead when things are going well, and you get punched in the mouth like that."

One game will not define Morris at OSU, nor should it. Bad games are bound to happen; Morris must prove they won't happen regularly.

OSU has shown a willingness to be patient with its head coaches. Morris' predecessor, Mike Gundy, got over two decades to lead OSU despite ending his first season with a 4-7 record. Gundy ultimately rewarded OSU's patience with years of consistency, and it makes sense to repeat the process with Morris.

In his first season coaching North Texas, Morris was blown out 58-21 by California in the season opener and finished 5-7. Two years later, North Texas was competing for a spot in the College Football Playoff. Maybe Morris will have a similar trajectory in Stillwater.

The week of the game, Morris stated that "Tulsa has our full attention," which made a lot of sense. TU's win last year was the final nail in the coffin for Gundy. TU head coach Tre Lamb called out OSU by saying, "We're going to beat their a-- again," during the offseason.

Despite the extra talent and motivation for this season's matchup, the game was extremely similar to last season. OSU's offense struggled much of the game, and the defense didn't get enough stops to keep them in it.

The Cowboys will play far more talented teams in the future, so their inability to keep up with TU is concerning for a team still searching for its first conference win in three years.

"On paper we got more resources, we got more history, we got all this stuff, and that's why they played the games, right?" Morris said. "So, for a second year in a row, they reign supreme on us right now on the football field, and that's a fact."

Whether due to poor offensive line play or just accuracy, Mestemaker missed throws against TU.

However, Mestemaker was expected to be the best quarterback OSU has had in the last couple of years, and that hype was not fabricated. He led the FBS in passing yards last season as a freshman and led his team to a conference championship game. His talent is still there; it is just about unlocking it.

He's also been praised for his leadership. Even after the loss, receiver Wyatt Young backed his quarterback.

"Drew's the most level-headed quarterback, level-headed guy I've ever met," Young said. "He's excited. He's optimistic. He never puts his head down. He's a leader. He knows that, so he never wants us to see him crashing out."

Oregon is a brutal opponent for the Cowboys to face as they try to turn their season around.

It embarrassed OSU last year in a 69-3 rout of Gundy's crew. After a 34-27 win against Boise State to start this year, the Ducks will want to get another statement win in Boone Pickens Stadium on Saturday.

Oregon ranked No. 2 in the AP preseason poll. Morris understands the challenge his team faces.

"We got a lot of problems to fix," Morris said. "[Oregon is] coming into town next week. And if they watch that film, they'll be licking their chops."

Maybe Saturday was just a bad first step. If it wasn't, the Cowboys are headed toward a much bigger problem than a loss to Tulsa.
