Headset Check: Momentum swings in Eric Morris’ favor

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-09-13
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Oklahoma State head coach Eric Morris and Oregon head coach Dan Lanning spent Saturday’s shootout fighting for control, but the Cowboys (1-1) were able to utilize the home-field advantage in their upset over the No. 6 Ducks (1-1).

The Cowboys' previous two seasons ended with 3-9 and 1-11 records. Add the fact that OSU lost to Tulsa the previous week, and the chances of the Cowboys beating the No. 6 team in the nation seemed slim.

The game also started 90 minutes late due to a weather delay. Despite the delay, OSU fans still showed up for their team. From the opening kickoff, the fans, band and everyone wearing orange were loud and rowdy. All the bad vibes during the week about the team seemed to have disappeared.

The Cowboys started strong, hoisting a 10-7 lead heading into their fifth drive on offense. The lead was exciting for fans, but with 11 minutes left in the second quarter, it could have evaporated quickly.

Morris decided not to play it safe. On the first play of the drive, quarterback Drew Mestemaker handed the ball off before it got pitched right back to him to throw to wide receiver Wyatt Young. The flea-flicker resulted in a 33-yard gain to start the eventual touchdown-scoring drive. The score put OSU up by 10 and reinforced the stadium's hope for an upset.

On the other sideline, Lanning was looking for a spark for Oregon.

With about seven minutes left in the fourth quarter, while OSU was up by five points, Oregon found itself in a fourth-and-8 situation on its own 33-yard line. Instead of flipping the field and trusting his defense to get another stop, Lanning decided to keep the offense on the field and attempt the fourth-down conversion.

Rolling out to his right, Oregon quarterback Dante Moore was pressured by multiple OSU defenders and ended up having to throw the ball on the ground in the area of running back Dierre Hill Jr. The incompletion made the stadium erupt in cheers, and the Cowboys used the field position to kick a field goal on their following drive, growing their lead to eight.

Morris and Lanning engaged in a chess match for much of the matchup. After the game, Morris gave his thoughts on Lanning’s decision.

“I thought just in those situations, I think he was trying to get something going and get some of the momentum on offense,” Morris said. “And credit to our defense for making those key stops and getting it back to our offense."

The situations for the two teams heading into Saturday were completely different. Oregon was ranked No. 6 in the country, while OSU was looking for its first FBS win in 22 tries. Despite the resumes, Morris and Lanning’s in-game decisions resulted in large momentum shifts for the fans in attendance, and Morris emerged on the winning side of that battle.

After the game, Morris was clear in his message to the fans who stormed the field.

“I've heard about [OSU’s home field advantage] all these years,” Morris said. “I've seen on the other side, like our student body, our paddle people, our band, all of our alumni. That was phenomenal. Hats off to you guys.”
