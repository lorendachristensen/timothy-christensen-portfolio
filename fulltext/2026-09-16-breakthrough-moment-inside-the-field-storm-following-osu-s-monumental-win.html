‘Breakthrough moment’: Inside the field storm following OSU’s monumental win

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-09-16
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

A lightning delay may have started the day, but the real storm hit after Oklahoma State’s 39-31 upset win over No. 6 Oregon on Saturday.

After quarterback Drew Mestemaker ran for the final first down of the game, Cowboy fans near the top of Boone Pickens Stadium began inching down the steps. They weren't heading toward an exit, but closer to the field of play.

With each kneel-down bleeding the final seconds off the clock, students leaned over the tall black barrier separating the stands and sidelines. It was no secret that pandemonium was about to ensue.

As Mestemaker knelt for the final play and threw the ball straight into the air, the barrier was broken. In a sea of orange, black and white, OSU fans and students rushed onto the turf, all making their way toward the logo at the center of the field. As time went on, the letters “OSU” disappeared under the mob, and little green remained visible on the field.

Among those rushing onto the field was OSU senior Scott Andersen, who described his experience during the field storm.

“There was a big rush, big migration as soon as it ended,” Andersen said. “People started kind of shoving, but for the most part, I think we were pretty chill about making sure people got down safely. Everyone ran to the center, kind of looking for the players. Started celebrating, taking photos and videos, and then I think I saw some people kind of head off toward the goalpost.”

Students did not leave the field empty-handed, as they tore down the west goalpost and carried its remains to Theta Pond. OSU freshman Zach Rubalcaba joined the effort to get it out of BPS.

“When the field goal started swaying down, I was like, ‘Okay, maybe I could help them,’” Rubalcaba said. “We had to go into the stands and go over the [southeast] fence. … So, me and a couple of the guys ran across the entire stadium to the exit to go on to the other side to bring it back down.”

The last time fans rushed BPS was in 2023 after the last Bedlam, when OSU beat Oklahoma 27-24. One OSU fan who stormed that field was a North Rock Creek high schooler named Caleb Hawkins.

Now that he is a star running back for the Cowboys, Hawkins got to enjoy the field storm as a player. He said postgame that he took full advantage of the opportunity.

“It's one of the best feelings I've ever felt,” Hawkins said. “Being able to score a touchdown in a big-time game, especially for my hometown, it's just a great feeling. … Being on the field and being one of the players to experience [a field storm], it was great. I got trapped in there for a while. I was probably one of the longest people in the pile. It was crazy.”

While Hawkins savored the moment, his teammate Mestemaker didn't get the same opportunity.

“The field storm was insane,” Mestemaker said. “I threw the ball up in the air, and then coach Brophy came up and hugged me, and then someone took my helmet, and then I was getting carried off the field by some cops to do some interview or something. So that was wild. But I mean, I'm so glad we got that done, and I'm so glad the fans got to experience that too.”

OSU officials announced Tuesday that the Big 12 would not impose its regular $50,000 fine for a field storm because OSU’s staff and security followed field-rushing safety protocols.

The postgame celebration was years in the making. In 2024, OSU began what became a 21-game losing streak against FBS opponents. In 2025, the program was embarrassed by the Ducks in a 69-3 rout.

The adversity continued for Cowboys fans at the start of this season. After losing to Tulsa in Week 1, a weather delay caused fans in attendance to wait an extra 90 minutes past the original kickoff time.

For students like Andersen, however, the past pain made the win against Oregon all the better.

“That win meant a lot to me as a fan because I knew it hasn't been the best few years for us,” Andersen said. “I think we've kind of had our tough go with things, but it meant a lot to me, after keeping up with the team for the last few years, to see a big breakthrough moment and kind of put OSU back in the national spotlight where it deserves to be.”

“I think the best thing about OSU I tell everyone is win or lose, Boone Pickens is gonna have a great environment. … They say loyal and true. Loyal and true means you're with them when they're terrible and lose 69-3 to Oregon, but you're also with them when they win 39-31. So, I'm sticking it out.”
