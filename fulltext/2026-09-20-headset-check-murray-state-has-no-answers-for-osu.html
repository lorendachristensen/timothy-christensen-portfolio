Headset Check: Murray State has no answers for OSU

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-09-20
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Murray State was overmatched from the opening play against Oklahoma State in the 59-0 beatdown.

After winning the coin toss, MSU head coach Jody Wright chose to receive the kickoff. It seemed he wanted to quiet the crowd early at Boone Pickens Stadium by scoring quickly. That plan did not come to fruition.

On the first play of the game, MSU (1-3) called a flea flicker. Quarterback Jim Ogle was pressured immediately and, instead of finding a receiver downfield, threw to pass-catcher Jaxon Potter for a 15-yard reception. The play didn't give Murray State the boost it hoped for and ended in a punt just three plays later.

The sequence foreshadowed the rest of the game. No matter what MSU tried, they could not overcome the physicality of the Cowboy defense. Whether it was a creative flea-flicker or standard run up the middle, MSU’s offense struggled to move down the field.

On the other side, OSU’s offense delivered an early touchdown for its home crowd. OSU head coach Eric Morris praised his offense after the game.

“In our first two drives, it wasn't just the explosive, big play,” Morris said. “I thought they were really good drives that showed consistency, running it, throwing it and marching it down the field. So, yeah, I think that was big. Builds some confidence."

The contrast to each team’s early approaches was noticeable, and the game played out that way. Morris was proud of his team for jumping out to an early lead.

“I think it's huge,” Morris said. “I think starting fast is something that's big, and gaining that momentum and then trying to keep it. We did a stat, I think it was two years ago, though, in college football that 73% of teams that had a lead at halftime won the game. That just shows you the importance of getting a lead and grabbing it and taking it into halftime. So, I thought our guys did start fast on both sides of the football.”

The Cowboys (2-1) will have a tougher time starting fast next week when they head to Morgantown to take on West Virginia.
