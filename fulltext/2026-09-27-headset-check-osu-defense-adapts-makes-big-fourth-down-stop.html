Headset Check: OSU defense adapts, makes big fourth down stop

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-09-27
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Oklahoma State defensive coordinator Skyler Cassity showed adaptability in OSU’s 41-24 win over West Virginia.

The Mountaineers (3-1, 0-1 Big 12) found lots of early success in the running game. In the first quarter alone, WVU ran for 131 yards. The offensive dominance was on full display when WVU took a 14-3 lead early in the second quarter, with quarterback Michael Hawkins Jr. completing only two passes for 16 yards.

Hawkins Jr. ran for 103 yards, but WVU running back Cam Cook caused the most problems for OSU’s (3-1, 1-0 Big 12) defense. Cook compiled 193 yards on the ground and seemed impossible to bring down.

It was OSU’s offense that kept the Cowboys in the game, as the team held a 28-24 lead late in the fourth quarter.

On third-and-1, OSU’s defense got a rare stop at the line of scrimmage against a Hawkins run. WVU head coach Rich Rodriguez decided to go for the fourth-and-1 on OSU’s 40-yard line, likely thinking that lightning wouldn’t strike twice.

Despite the seemingly sound logic that went with the decision, it ultimately backfired for Rodriguez’s squad. Cam Cook was immediately met by OSU defenders and was brought down for a 2-yard loss on the fourth-down try.

The stop marked a turning point for OSU's run defense. Cassity’s defense allowed only 10 rushing yards and zero points for the remaining 16 minutes of the game. Head coach Eric Morris spoke on the significance of the play.

“The fourth-down stop was huge,” Morris said. “And I think at that moment in the game, the crowd was still into it. And I thought that was where you could kind of see them deflate a little bit, and our guys kind of go and take it at that point.”

The Cowboys appeared to make a concerted effort to commit extra defenders to the box as the game progressed. While that left one-on-one matchups on the perimeter, it forced WVU to prove it could consistently beat OSU through the air.

Besides the schematic adjustments, a simple explanation for the turnaround was that the Cowboys just missed fewer tackles in the second half. Morris spoke on what he saw from the unit.

“Coaches did a phenomenal job at halftime making some adjustments,” Morris said. “Really opportunistic second half for our defense, and I told them when we left the locker room, ‘the toughest team is going to win this game,’ and [I’m] just really happy about the way that we responded.”

WVU won the battle on the ground for most of the night, but OSU won the moment when it mattered most.
