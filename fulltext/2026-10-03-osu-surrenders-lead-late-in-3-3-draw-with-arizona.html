OSU surrenders lead late in 3-3 draw with Arizona

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-10-03
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

A late Arizona penalty kick spoiled Oklahoma State's bid for a Big 12 victory in a high-scoring 3-3 draw Friday night.

The longest goal drought came in the second half after OSU (5-4-3, 1-2-1 Big 12) scored its third goal of the day. The Cowgirls held a one-goal lead for 34 minutes and were less than four minutes away from the win.

Arizona (3-5-4, 0-1-3 Big 12) kept fighting.

In an effort to chase the ball, OSU’s Sophie Wilson made hard contact with Arizona’s Zoe Mendiola on a kick. The act gave Wilson a yellow card and Arizona a free kick just outside of the penalty box. Wilson then made another mistake by hitting the ball with her hand on Ella Weathersby’s free kick, giving the Wildcats a penalty kick opportunity.

Weathersby took advantage of the second chance with a game-tying goal in the 87th minute. The goal drained the energy from the crowd at Neal Patterson Stadium.

The final 3-3 score felt like a letdown for an OSU team that controlled the majority of the match. Head coach Colin Carmichael expressed his mixed feelings with the result.

“It's kind of a crazy game, right,” Carmichael said. “Had a little bit of everything, and you know, disappointed that we didn't get the win. Probably the fact that they scored so late on the PK makes it feel a little bit like a loss. But ultimately, it was a tight game… I thought we were really good, and then we sort of lost our way, and Arizona came into the game… They fought back and didn't give up. So yeah, disappointing in the tie, but ultimately it was a very tight game and typical Big 12 soccer match.”

The Cowgirls never spent a moment trailing in the matchup, forcing the Wildcats to be the hunters much of the night.

After OSU’s Emmy Hedden scored the opening goal on a penalty kick, Arizona answered just four minutes later. Mireya Stephenson scored the goal after a perfectly placed lob pass from Zoe Mendiola.

The Cowgirls finished the first half strong with an Emily Senatore kick from the left side of the field just outside the box, and she was able to sneak it into the bottom right corner of the net.

However, Arizona was able to respond quickly once again. Three minutes into the second half, Lily Boydston converted on a free kick in almost the same area that Senatore scored her goal.

The Cowgirls’ inability to pull away left the door open for the late Arizona score, and that was a possibility Carmichael saw coming.

“They didn't quit,” Carmichael said. “When we got them down, I did say to our staff on the bench at 3-2, I didn't feel comfortable the way this game was going. You felt like there was more goals there, and we had a couple of chances. Maybe we could have put them away, but unfortunately we didn't. So no, I did not feel comfortable, and they showed a lot of toughness to fight back and get those goals to get the tie on the road. So I give them great, great credit as well.”

The draw likely stings more after the Cowgirls went 0-2 on their road trip against Baylor and Houston. As the season continues, Carmichael and his team may wish they had secured some of those early wins in what is proving to be a tough Big 12 conference.

The Cowgirls will hope to get back on track when they welcome Texas Tech to Stillwater on Thursday.
