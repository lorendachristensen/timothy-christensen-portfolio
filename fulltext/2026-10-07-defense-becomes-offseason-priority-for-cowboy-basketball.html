Defense becomes offseason priority for Cowboy basketball

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-10-07
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Despite boasting one of the Big 12's most productive offenses last season, Oklahoma State coaches and players know they need improvement heading into this season.

Last season, OSU ranked second in the Big 12 in points per game, behind Arizona. The prolific scoring helped the Cowboys win several nonconference games, but success became harder to find once Big 12 play began. OSU went 6-12 in conference play and missed the NCAA tournament as a result.

Head coach Steve Lutz and his staff identified defense as the team's biggest issue. OSU surrendered 83.2 points a game, the worst in the Big 12. They have spent the offseason attempting to solve the problem.

Sophomore Mekhi Ragland was at OSU last season and has noticed changes in this year’s camp.

“I just think we've spent a lot more time defensively in the summer, in those 10 practices,” Ragland said. “We were really hammering defense every day. The first hour, we're not touching a ball. So I just think we're way farther along defensively.”

Changing the practice is one way to increase defensive intensity, but OSU also needed players who would fit the shift in identity.

Associate head coach James Miller, who plays a significant role in recruiting, targeted players known for their defensive ability during the offseason.

Miller has noticed players buying into the philosophy.

“I think the majority of the guys, most everybody, has been on board because that's been a point of emphasis,” Miller said. “But Kashie Natt, Anthony Felesi, those guys are just freaks, man. Those guys are consistent motors, energy guys who are elite defenders, and then you have a couple of other guys. Kanye [Clary] is really engaged. Jordan Burks is another one. When those guys get to flying around, it's just infectious to the rest of our team.”

Mentioning Natt and Felesi in the same sentence is potentially exciting for Cowboy basketball fans. Natt is a fifth-year player who comes from Sam Houston, where he won Conference USA Defensive Player of the Year. Felesi is still an unproven freshman, but OSU hopes he can develop into a similarly impactful defender.

The Big 12 looks like a gauntlet once again, and pressure is building for the Lutz-led team to break through for a tournament appearance. Miller voiced the team’s determination for progress this season.

“Look, if we would have been able to guard last year, we would have been in the NCAA tournament,” Miller said. “I don't think anybody in this group feels differently. So I think coaches made the adjustment in the mindset, and not to say that we weren't trying to get them to play defense last year, we just wouldn't do it.

“… [This season], I think everybody’s bought into, ‘Hey, we have enough talent to score, and if we can defend and grab a few rebounds, then we'll be where we need to be come March,’ and so that's been the mentality of our group.”
