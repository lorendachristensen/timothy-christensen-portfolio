Wyatt Young brings production, passion to OSU’s receiving corps

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-10-14
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Oklahoma State wide receiver Wyatt Young brought his North Texas production to Stillwater early in the season.

Young enjoyed a breakout season at UNT last year. During the sophomore’s campaign, Young compiled 70 receptions, 1,264 receiving yards and 10 receiving touchdowns en route to an American Conference Championship appearance. He received All-American Conference First-Team honors.

Young followed his head coach Eric Morris and quarterback Drew Mestemaker to OSU this season. Despite the jump to Power Four play, he is picking up right where he left off.

Through five games, Young has 27 receptions, 462 receiving yards and four touchdowns. After losing the season opener to Tulsa, the receiver has found the end zone in his last four games.

Young’s best game came on the road in Morgantown, West Virginia, where a second-half explosion led to eight receptions for a career-high 192 yards against WVU. The performance helped the Cowboys get their first Big 12 win in over two years.

Another factor in OSU's success has been the energy Young brings to the team. Young talks to his teammates, but also to his opponents, drawing agitated, animated reactions from players in non-orange jerseys.

Young claims he only offers words of encouragement to his opponents and quickly points out that he doesn't receive penalty flags for his in-game chats.

“I keep it in between the whistles,” Young said. “I try my best to, at least.”

Morris, who played wide receiver at Texas Tech, explained how he handles a player like Young.

“It’s hard for me at times,” Morris said. “I was the same way as a player, and so I remember having to be reined in at times. And so, yeah, we've definitely had some conversations. Obviously, you don't want to take that passion that he plays with out.

“I think our team feeds off of it, and at the same time, he's got to be smart and never hurt this team by getting a penalty at some point, right? I've always thought it's better to have to pull them back a little bit as opposed to push them.”

Offensive lineman Ashton Lepo shared how Young’s style energizes the group.

“It's great to see that,” Lepo said. “I think up front, you got to be a gritty, gritty group of guys, and like we're just chippy. And I look over there, and I see Wyatt doing it. It's like infectious; it gets me going, man. It's good. I love seeing that stuff.”

As the team prepares to take on No. 20 Houston on the road, Young has high expectations for his group.

“Finally, allowing us to hopefully make a statement win,” Young said. “Go out there and compete with the best, and hopefully whip up on the best. Just being able to go out there and show that we are a legit team, and finally people will start taking us seriously, hopefully.”

Young showed his underdog mentality when asked whether he feels the team has been disrespected.

“I feel like we all have,” Young said. “I mean, our motto is misfits. We've all been kind of disrespected our whole career, under-recruited, or just people don't think we're as good as we are. But I think getting a major win under our belt like this, finally, people can start putting us higher in the rankings and whatever.”
