Cowboys unable to overcome early struggles in 35-32 loss to Houston

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-10-18
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

HOUSTON — The 18th-ranked Oklahoma State Cowboys made inopportune mistakes that cost them a 35-32 loss to No. 20 Houston at TDECU Stadium.

Cougar quarterback Conner Weigman had his way against the Cowboys’ defense, completing 24-of-33 passes for 343 yards and five touchdowns, plus 52 rushing yards.

The Cougars came into the weekend with the No. 1 third-down offense, and they had a lot of success Saturday night as well. UH converted seven of its 13 third-down attempts and all three fourth-down attempts, scoring touchdowns on two plays.

With 4:54 remaining, OSU scored a touchdown to cut UH’s lead to three. Weigman converted two deep third-down passes on the Cougars’ final drive and kept OSU off the field.

The Cowboys started strong. On the opening drive, Weigman’s pass bounced off DJ Jackson’s helmet and into defensive back LaDanian Fields’ hands, resulting in the Cougars’ only turnover of the game.

All of quarterback Drew Mestemaker’s first-half completions were for 10 yards or more. The issues came when the field shortened for the Cowboys. The Cowboys’ first two drives put them inside the 5-yard line but yielded only three combined points.

Mestemaker finished 22-for-34 for 365 passing yards but also committed two costly turnovers. Mestemaker threw an interception in the second quarter that was returned to the OSU 25-yard line. In the fourth quarter, Mestemaker lost a fumble on a sack, slowing momentum for a comeback.

Mestemaker talked about not getting to attempt a game-winning drive.

“I mean, I was hoping that we got a chance,” Mestemaker said. “It sucks staying on the sideline watching the game end, and in that moment, you can’t do anything about it. But then, you also got to look back, like we scored three points in the first half. So, it’s like I’m not one to point fingers.”

Turnover and red-zone struggles have plagued the Cowboys all season, but those problems proved fatal Saturday. Halftime adjustments have been key for OSU in Big 12 play, and the Cowboys outscored Houston 29-21 in the second half, but it wasn’t enough to win.

Morris still believes that his team can bounce back.

“I mean, we don’t have any choice,” Morris said. “Like we don’t have time to feel sorry for ourselves, and nobody’s going to feel sorry for us. I feel like we have a really good ball club right now, and we’re building something right now, and so I’m happy where we’re at.

“Obviously, not happy with the result today, and a bunch of those guys, you could tell on their faces how much that meant to them, which I think is a sign of us getting somewhere right now.”
