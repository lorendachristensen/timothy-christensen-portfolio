Headset Check: Fourth-down outcomes hurt OSU

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-10-18
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

HOUSTON – Oklahoma State head coach Eric Morris’ team struggled in key moments early in its 35-32 loss to Houston.

The Cowboys (4-2, 2-1 Big 12) moved the ball early on the Cougars (6-1, 3-1 Big 12). After cornerback LaDainian Fields intercepted a tipped pass from Houston quarterback Conner Weigman early in the first quarter, OSU started its opening drive on UH’s 48-yard line. OSU quarterback Drew Mestemaker seemed completely in control and marched the offense to the 5-yard line.

The drive stalled, and OSU settled for a field goal on fourth-and-goal.

The Cowboys’ next possession ended similarly, with a 75-yard drive losing steam at the 2-yard line. Morris decided not to repeat his decision from the last drive, opting for a fourth-down attempt.

Throwing in traffic, Mestemaker could not complete the touchdown pass to wide receiver Wyatt Young. The Cowboys had scored only three points in two tries inside the 5-yard line, creating a hole they never climbed out of.

Morris lamented how the fourth-down decisions affected the game.

“The first two drives, we marched right down the field and can't find a way to get points,” Morris said. “And so hindsight's 20/20… I chased some points again. If I kick that field goal early, here we are. We're a tie ball game and find a way to get into overtime, and so that's something that I gotta soul search on.”

The decision had its risks and rewards. Kicking a second field goal so close to the end zone was less than ideal, and a touchdown would have swung momentum in OSU’s favor.

Perhaps the biggest issue was not the decision, but the play itself. The offense had a timeout between quarters to come up with a call it liked, but ultimately it led to zero points.

Mestemaker shared his thoughts on OSU’s red-zone struggles.

“I guess you can put it all into the same category of not executing,” Mestemaker said. “It's not like we're not strong enough to be down there. It's not like the other team is pulling out these magical plays. We just got to execute the play call at the end of the day.”

Whatever the issue, Morris and his staff will want to find better ways to finish drives. Otherwise, the Cowboys will continue leaving points on the field.
