Kashie Natt shines in exhibition win over Tulsa

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-10-23
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Sam Houston transfer Kashie Natt took the opportunity to showcase his talents in Oklahoma State’s 93-85 exhibition win over Tulsa.

Natt played 14 minutes in OSU’s first exhibition against New Mexico. With fellow fifth-years Kanye Clary and Parsa Fallah out, OSU leaned heavily on Natt’s leadership and energy in his 33 minutes against the Golden Hurricane.

The guard finished the afternoon one assist short of a triple-double, with 25 points, 12 rebounds and 9 assists. Natt’s production did not stop on the offensive end, leading the team with five steals.

Natt also provided the pre-homecoming crowd at Gallagher-Iba Arena with plenty of energy. After a skillful layup in the early second half, Natt turned toward the crowd to celebrate the play, raising the noise decibels in the building.

The crowd also cheered later in the half after Natt’s fifth steal of the game, when he acted out the seatbelt “lockdown” celebration.

The new Cowboy enjoyed the arena environment he got to play in.

“Man, it felt great,” Natt said. “I ain't gonna lie. I'm not used to playing in front of this many people. Even if it probably wasn't a lot to most people, but it was a lot to me. That energy just made me play harder for the team, for the people in the stands, just to give them a show.”

Head coach Steve Lutz brought Natt to Stillwater to improve upon last year’s Cowboy defense. Natt earned Conference USA Defensive Player of the Year last season at Sam Houston.

Natt’s production on the offensive side might have been a shock to many, but not for teammate Jacob Walker.

“Kash[ie] doesn't surprise me anymore,” Walker said. “It's like I've seen probably seen way more crazier plays [from Natt], but Kash is a guy that his energy is contagious. So once he gets us going, it's easy for everybody else to get gritty and do the rest.”

Lutz was also not surprised by Natt’s performance.

“Yeah, I mean, I watch him every day in practice,” Lutz said. “I've told y'all before he's special, right? I mean, his energy and his effort is contagious. It doesn't stop. It's every single day, whether it's practice at 7 a.m. or 7 p.m. … You want him on your team no matter where you're at.”
