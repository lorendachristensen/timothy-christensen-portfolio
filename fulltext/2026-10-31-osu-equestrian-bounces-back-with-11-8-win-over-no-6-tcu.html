OSU equestrian bounces back with 11-8 win over No. 6 TCU

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-10-31
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Seventh-ranked Oklahoma State earned its first win of the season on Friday, beating No. 6 TCU 11-8.

OSU (1-1 overall, 1-1 Big 12) lost its season opener 11-8 on the road against Fresno State on Oct. 17. Head coach Larry Sanchez said that his team wanted back on the winning path.

“We were a little disappointed about our performance at Fresno, and the girls were really looking forward to getting back into the arena and showing what they can do,” Sanchez said. “Very excited about the win over a very tough TCU team.”

The last time OSU faced the Horned Frogs, the Cowgirls earned a 14-5 victory in the 2026 Big 12 Championship.

Much of the day went smoothly for the Cowgirls. Despite early rain, the Cowgirls won their first two events, 3-2.

The Fences event was held outside, but the rain did not slow the Cowgirls down. Emma Hechtman was the highest-scoring rider in the event, posting a 92.5 from the judges and earning Most Outstanding Player. TCU’s (3-3 overall, 2-1 Big 12) Amy Krebs posted the second-highest score with 90, just beating out OSU’s Lauren Gee’s 89.5.

Horsemanship also went to the Cowgirls. Kenzie Bracken, Alana Stanton and Piper Anderson all earned points for OSU. The event was evenly matched, with three of the five matchups being decided by fewer than two points.

Reigning was even tighter, ending in a 2-2 tie. TCU’s top two scorers, Calla Thomas and Adele Cunningham, earned wins in the event. Thomas was the only Horn Frog to earn a MOP award.

OSU ended the day with a 3-2 win in the Flat competition. OSU’s Caroline Mirts led the scoring, but Sophia Tabasso took home the MOP award for the Cowgirls.

It was a rainy day, but it did not hurt Sanchez’s team.

“We've been preparing the girls for it since the forecast came out last week, and just telling them, you know what: We're Cowgirl tough,” Sanchez said. “And we're going to ride through it if we can, as long as there's not lightning. We're going to keep pushing through.

“In a way, it's kind of an answered prayer because that top arena is better when it's got moisture in it, the outdoor arena. And so by us getting a little bit of rain this morning actually made the footing better for the horses, and I think the girls and the horses and everybody appreciated it.”

The OSU fans in attendance were also not deterred by the weather and showed up for their team. Sanchez was appreciative of the Friday crowd.

“I’ll tell you what, the more orange we have in the stands, the higher level the girls ride at,” Sanchez said. “I mean, they really feed off of the energy that the fans bring, and we can't thank the fans enough for coming out and supporting.”

Sanchez and his team will now turn their attention to SMU and Georgia, which OSU will face in Dallas next week.
