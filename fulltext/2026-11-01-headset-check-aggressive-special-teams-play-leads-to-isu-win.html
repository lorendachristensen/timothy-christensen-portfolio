Headset Check: Aggressive Special Teams play leads to ISU win

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-11-01
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

The scoreboard operator had a busy Halloween night in No. 24 Oklahoma State’s 51-49 loss to Iowa State.

Both offenses produced at a high level for most of the night. The teams combined for 1,114 yards and 100 points in the game. OSU (5-3, 3-2 Big 12) averaged 7.2 yards per play, while ISU (4-4, 2-3 Big 12) averaged 8.2.

With a limited number of defensive stops, the outcome was going to come down to execution in critical situations.

ISU head coach Jimmy Rogers knew what he was preparing for. OSU quarterback Drew Mestemaker has received national recognition for his abilities, and he is surrounded by playmakers including Caleb Hawkins, Wyatt Young and Justin Bowick. The unit has rarely been slowed this season, so Rogers decided to try to steal a possession.

Early in the second quarter, a Jaylen Raynor pass to Easton Miller tied the game up for the Cyclones at 14 apiece. Instead of doing the routine kickoff, Rogers’ team attempted a surprise onside kick.

The Cowboys were unprepared, and kicker Kyle Konrardy recovered his own kick at the 48-yard line, forcing the OSU offense to remain on the sideline.

Two plays later, ISU found itself in a third-and-1 situation. The OSU defense had the chance to make up for the special teams’ blunder and get the ball back to its offense. Instead, Raynor found Jamal Polite for a 41-yard touchdown, giving the Cyclones a 21-14 lead.

The surprise onside kick was the first of two special teams giveaways by the Cowboys, the other occurring 10 minutes later due to a muffed punt by Bobby Deal Jr.

Rogers said he knew early in the week the Cyclones would eventually attempt an onside kick.

“I planned on it all week,” Rogers said. “I knew we had to steal a possession at some point, and I was kind of just waiting. I almost did it on the opening kickoff, but I thought, you know, I’ll wait till we score.

“And we scored quickly, and kind of battled back and forth. And when we got the opportunity again, I felt like it was the right timing, and it just panned out perfectly. So I’m happy for the kick. Obviously, it was well executed, and he does it all the time in practice.”

The extra possessions worked perfectly for the Cyclones and helped the team outduel OSU’s high-powered offense.
