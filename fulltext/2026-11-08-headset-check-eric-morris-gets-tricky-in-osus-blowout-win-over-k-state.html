Headset Check: Eric Morris gets tricky in OSU's blowout win over K-State

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-11-08
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

With the game already in hand, Oklahoma State head coach Eric Morris reached into his bag of tricks.

The OSU (6-3, 4-2 Big 12) offense had its highest-scoring game of the season in a 66-28 win over Kansas State (4-5, 1-5 Big 12) in the ‘Little Apple.’

The OSU offense has found success all season in moving the ball. The unit ranked third in the country in yards per game with nearly 530 yards. Quarterback Drew Mestemaker has led the charge, passing for almost 339 yards per game.

The Achilles’ heel for the unit has been its red-zone execution. The offense had scored a touchdown on only 26 of its 46 red-zone trips, something that Morris has said he wanted to fix.

With four minutes left in the third quarter, OSU found itself up 56-21 and at the 5-yard line in the red zone. Morris wanted to run up the score, and he had the play to do it.

In a play similar to the ‘Philly Special’ run by the Eagles in the 2018 Super Bowl, a jet sweep-reverse resulted in receiver Wyatt Young passing to a wide-open Mestemaker, who walked into the end zone. Morris explained the history of the play and took a playful shot at Young.

“It's been in for a while,” Morris said. “It's just something that we carry when people want to pressure and go man-to-man coverage down there. I think my first ever big win at Incarnate Word, that play sealed the whole entire game with us.

“… I think you know, obviously, during that time I thought one more score would be really good for us. What they were doing down there once we got tight, their angle was to bring some zero pressure. Luckily, it wasn't a great ball by Wyatt, but it got there.”

The play gave Young his first passing touchdown of his career and came a week after Young apologized for a late unsportsmanlike penalty in OSU’s loss to Iowa State.

The score was also Mestemaker’s sixth and final total touchdown of the game. He became the first Big 12 player to score four passing, one rushing and one receiving touchdown in a single game.
