OSU receiver Justin Bowick turns long journey into breakout season

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-11-11
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

After stops at three previous programs, Oklahoma State wide receiver Justin Bowick finally found success in Stillwater.

Bowick has been a top target for quarterback Drew Mestemaker and the OSU offense. Few expected Bowick to emerge as OSU’s leading receiver, but he has caught 44 passes for 790 yards.

The fifth-year player’s biggest strength is his ability to find the end zone. He has 10 receiving touchdowns through the first three quarters of the 2026 season. Bowick has caught a touchdown in every game this season except the Week 2 matchup against Oregon.

Bowick’s production has helped bolster one of the nation’s best offenses. OSU is second in total yards this season, just 10 yards behind Mestemaker’s former team, North Texas.

It hasn’t always been as easy as Bowick has made it look this year. His jump to stardom was a long and strenuous process.

Coming out of high school, Bowick was a zero-star recruit after recording 11 receptions for 131 yards and one touchdown as a senior. The Atlanta native decided to play football at Eastern Illinois.

Bowick played in eight games as a freshman and 11 as a sophomore. He finished his two seasons at EIU with 43 receptions for 709 yards and eight touchdowns.

Bowick transferred to Ball State in his third year, where he showed flashes of his potential in an injury-riddled year. He appeared in four games, preserved a redshirt season, and recorded more than 140 receiving yards twice.

Bowick then moved up to the Power Four at Illinois, where he struggled to contribute. He played in 12 games but never had a performance with over 50 receiving yards.

After Illinois, Bowick wanted a fourth team in as many years to call home. Head coach Eric Morris was building a revamped team at OSU and believed that Bowick could fit into his system before the season.

“I think Justin Bowick’s a guy that has a skill set to play this game for a long time,” Morris said. “He has the length and speed. I think he had our fastest timed 10-meter dash this summer at six foot five. So a guy that you know is looking at a 4.43 guy at that size is a guy [I’m] super excited about.”

Bowick’s physical traits helped bring him to Stillwater, but he still had work to do to earn playing time.

Wyatt Young and Miles Coleman were two other receiver transfers that joined OSU in the offseason. Both players were at North Texas with Morris and Mestemaker in the nation-leading passing offense. Those relationships were already formed before Bowick got to Stillwater.

Something that sets Bowick apart from other receivers is his ability and willingness to block. He has made contact with numerous defensive backs this season, being the catalyst for some explosive runs.

Bowick showed his work ethic when building reps with Mestemaker in the offseason.

“I always say the first day I got here, we ran routes,” Bowick said. “We just stacked from the first day, spring, summer all the way until now, and I feel like we’re having a really good [run] right now.”

Morris saw the progress before the results, and complimented Bowick before the season.

“He’s worked hard this whole entire summer,” Morris said. “… Almost every time I went in [the facility after practice], Justin Bowick’s in there, whether he’s catching jugs, working on his craft. I think he’s just an older kid, but he’s super committed to the processes of getting better.

“And I think you’ve really been able to see that pay off. Now, he’s got to do it in games now, right, when it counts the most. But I think his transition and catching ability have improved so much over the course of the last four or five months [and] is something that I’ve been excited about.”

Bowick is putting it all together in the games. He was added to the Biletnikoff Award Watch List on Nov. 5, which is given to the most outstanding pass-catcher in the FBS.
