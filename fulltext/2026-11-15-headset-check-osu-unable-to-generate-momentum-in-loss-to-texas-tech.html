Headset Check: OSU unable to generate momentum in loss to Texas Tech

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-11-15
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Oklahoma State was never able to overcome its sluggish start, falling to Texas Tech 52-27.

After winning the coin toss, head coach Eric Morris decided to defer receiving the ball to the second half. The decision has been Morris' preference this season when given the option.

The choice has its rewards. Getting the ball in the second half gives your team an extra possession midgame, allowing teams to get a feel for the game before scripting a drive.

Deferring the kickoff also has its risks, as OSU learned Saturday night. Texas Tech’s Malcolm Simmons returned the opening kickoff 97 yards for a touchdown. The play immediately took the energy out of the blackout crowd.

The Red Raiders set the tone for the game on the kickoff return against the struggling Cowboys. Texas Tech later took an early 21-0 lead in the first quarter. Morris voiced his frustrations on the early score.

“It started off on the wrong foot,” Morris said. “I mean, we knew that they were going to run a field return. That's what we've struggled with this year, and we talked about communication was one of our goals, and the communication was so poor on the very first play of the game on our kickoff coverage, and [TTU] capitalized on it… We just never were able to capture the momentum back.”

The Cowboys showed signs of life in the second quarter, cutting the deficit to 28-20 by halftime.

Coming out of the break, OSU had a chance to take advantage of its decision to defer the opening possession. OSU could set a new tone early in the second half.

OSU found itself at third-and-1 on its own 46-yard line. After an incomplete pass and a stuffed run attempt, OSU turned the ball over on downs.

The Red Raiders took advantage quickly, with quarterback Tommy Castellanos throwing a 45-yard passing touchdown on the next play, extending a lead that only grew for the rest of the night.

“I mean, I think us coming back out after halftime and really going to score and allowing us to get the crowd back, energized and stuff at that time, would have been a huge deal for us,” Morris said. “Weren't able to get that going and just super, super stale in my opinion in the second half.”

The missed opportunities caused Morris to suffer his first home loss as OSU's head coach.
