Headset Check: OSU survives Arizona State’s fourth-down perfection

By Timothy Christensen  /  The O'Colly  /  2026-11-22
This is an archived copy preserved for Timothy Christensen's portfolio. The original was published in The O'Colly at this link; this copy is shown because the original page is no longer reachable.

Head coach Eric Morris and Oklahoma State overcame Arizona State’s success on all five fourth-down conversions in the Cowboys’ 30-27 overtime win.

ASU (6-5, 4-4 Big 12) bounced back from its blowout loss to No. 12 Texas Tech on Oct. 17, winning three of its last four games.

Head coach Kenny Dillingham led ASU’s dramatic turnaround, and he is nearing the end of his fourth season with the Sun Devils. His tenure includes a Big 12 Coach of the Year award and a Big 12 Championship win in 2024.

Dillingham is known for being aggressive on fourth downs. Going into Saturday night, ASU had attempted 24 fourth-down conversions and was successful 58.3% of the time, ranking third in the Big 12.

It was not set to be a great matchup for the Cowboys (7-4, 5-3 Big 12), who have given up the most fourth-down conversions in the conference (14).

The coach brought that aggressiveness into the first half. ASU converted two fourth-and-1 opportunities during an 11-play second-quarter drive to take a 14-7 lead.

ASU later faced a fourth-and-10 with 12 seconds left in the half. Dillingham kept the punt team on the sideline and went for it on OSU’s 45-yard line. Quarterback Jake Fette took advantage of the Cowboys’ soft coverage, finding Reed Harris just beyond the line to gain.

The conversion helped set up a 51-yard field goal from ASU’s Carson Smith to end the half, giving the Sun Devils a 24-7 lead.

The second half was a different story. OSU won the third quarter 10-0 and threatened a comeback in the fourth quarter.

As quarterback Drew Mestemaker led the OSU offense for a game-tying drive, they faced a fourth-and-1 on the ASU 7-yard line. Morris knew he couldn't settle for a field goal and called a run up the middle with Caleb Hawkins. A hole opened up, and Hawkins scored a game-tying touchdown.

The Cowboys later forced overtime. OSU got the ball first, but its drive stalled at the 17-yard line. Morris decided to take the points on fourth-and-2 and sent Sam Keltner to the field to kick a field goal.

Morris’ conservative call paid off, as Quinton Hammond intercepted Fette’s pass to end the game.
